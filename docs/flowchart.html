How dedup.py works

Finding the same Family twice

The script reads an Archdiocese-wide ParishSoft export and looks for Family records that are really one household. It also keeps apart Families that share a person but are different households, such as after a divorce or when a grown child has their own Family. Every match carries a confidence level and plain-language reasons.

The pipeline

Seven steps from spreadsheets to merge list

  1. 1

    Read three exports

    joined by Family DUID
    Families159,235names, address, phones, email, parish, status, dates
    Members381,561names, birthdate, role, deceased, cell, email, edits
    Enhanced Families159,233"last updated" date and emergency phone; 349 broken rows skipped
  2. 2

    Clean every value

    300 placeholder Families dropped

    Records are put into one comparable form before anything is compared.

    "126 N. Birchwood Avenue" = "126 N Birchwood" Bill = William, Pat = Patrick or Patricia Patricia ≠ Patrick, Juan ≠ Juana O'Brien = OBrien "no email", "UNLISTED", "N/A" treated as blank Loose Collection, Unknown Family skipped
  3. 3

    Pick candidate pairs

    395,421 pairs to compare

    Comparing every Family with every other would mean about 12.7 billion pairs. Instead, two records are compared only if they share one of these:

    an emaila phone (home, cell, emergency)a street addresssurname + ZIPa person: same name + exact birthdate

    The last key finds Families that moved and changed every address, phone and parish.

  4. 4

    Score the Family records

    base confidence

    Surname (same, or similar such as a typo or compound name), first names (same, overlapping, or different), and how many contacts they share: email, phone, address. Contact info shared by more than three surnames, such as a nursing home or parish office, doesn't count.

  5. 5

    Check the Members

    46,044 kept · 23,746 kept apart

    The people in each Family confirm, raise, lower or overrule the base score. The checks run in a fixed order, shown in the decision ladder below.

  6. 6

    Build duplicate groups

    23,157 groups

    Matches join into groups, strongest evidence first. A link is skipped if it would put two Families that were kept apart, or that fail the same checks when compared directly, into the same group. A record left out of every group is listed on a separate sheet for review.

  7. 7

    Choose a survivor and write the workbook

    32,056 Families to merge

    Each group keeps one record and lists the others as (survivor, merged) pairs ready for ParishSoft's merge tool. The workbook has five sheets: Duplicates, Merge Pairs, Reasons, Not Grouped and Summary.

Special cases

Life events the rules account for

Example names are invented.

Moved to a new parish

AJohn & Mary Reyes · Inactive · old parish
BJohn & Mary Reyes · Active · new parish, new address
Merge into the Active parish

Same people by name and birthdate, even though every contact changed. The most common case: 18,360 merges.

Divorce and remarriage

AJohn + Mary + their children
BJohn + Susan
Kept apart

John is in both, but each Family has a living partner the other doesn't.

Widowed and remarried

AJohn + Mary (deceased)
BJohn + Susan
Merge, full confidence

The only living adult in A lives in B, and both Mary and Susan are of John's generation (a late parent, or a grown child, doesn't count). Deaths are read from the deceased flag, date of death, Member Status or a "Dec." type.

Ex-spouse's own household

ATom + Ann (typed "Ex-Wife")
BAnn + children
Kept apart

An ex-spouse who matches the other Family's head marks two households.

Grown child with their own Family

AParents + Sam (Child or "Adult", age 34)
BSam (Head) + spouse
Kept apart

Works on a name-only match too. "Unknown"/"Adult" Members under 18, or 18+ years younger than another Member, count as children.

Parent moved in with a child

ARose (Head), her own record
BDaughter + husband + Rose ("Mother")
Kept apart

Head in one Family, parent in the other, with no head or spouse in both.

Separated parents

AMom + Ella + Max
BDad + Ella + Max
Kept apart

The children match exactly, but each Family has a parent the other lacks.

Father and son, same name

AJohn Smith, b. 1950
BJohn Smith, b. 1985, same street
Kept apart

Different birthdates, or Jr. vs Sr. One-digit or swapped-digit typos are still treated as one person.

Spouse recorded differently

AMark + Terri Dunn (née Lowe)
BMark + Teri Lowe, birthdates swapped
Merge

A spouse counts as present if a spouse in the other record has the same name, middle name, exact birthdate or a one-letter spelling variant.

Single record vs married record

AJohn alone
BJohn + Susan
Merge, one level lower

This could be a record from before the marriage (a duplicate) or after a divorce (not), so it is flagged with lower confidence. If both spouses have single records, both can merge into the couple's record.

Step 7 in detail

Which record survives

Survivor ranking, first difference wins

  1. Registered = Yes
  2. Active group, so a Family that moved merges into the parish where it is Active
  3. Has a living Member
  4. Most recent real activity: membership date, unregistered date, Family "last updated", or a Member edit
  5. Most fields filled in
  6. Lowest DUID (tie-breaker)

Bulk dates are ignored

Mass cleanups stamp thousands of records at once, which would make them look recently active. A date doesn't count as activity if it falls on:

  • a day with 500+ unregistrations or updates Archdiocese-wide (e.g. 14,869 on 11 Dec 2024)
  • a day with 100+ in a single parish
  • a minute with 200+ Member edits (the 2023–24 conversions)

What each level means

LevelTypical evidence
Very highTwo or more people match by name and exact birthdate, or same surname and first names with shared contact info
HighSame surname, overlapping first names ("David" vs "David & Kim") and one shared contact; or one adult matches exactly with no shared contact
MediumSame names in the same ZIP with no shared contact (likely a move); a similar surname; only children match exactly
Low (review)Different first names at one address, one Family with an extra adult, or a link through other records in a large group. Review before merging.

Results

Data quirks handled along the way

  • Catch-all records: 25 Families whose Members have six or more surnames, such as a certificate holding record, give no Member evidence.
  • Placeholder Members: entries whose first name is the surname (contribution records) are ignored, unless they have a birthdate, a nickname or a specific role such as Daughter.
  • Shared contact info: an address, phone or email used by more than three surnames, such as a nursing home or parish office, isn't treated as evidence.
  • Broken rows: 349 shifted rows in the enhanced export and 3 in the Family export are skipped.
  • Suspect birth years: about 35 records in one parish show 1999–2001 where other records show 1937–43. They are treated as different people.
